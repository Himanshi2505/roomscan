# Fix-loop declaration

Committed **before** the fix. Before = tag `fix-before`, after = tag `fix-after`.
Regenerate both with `python tools/fixloop_ceiling.py` (laser ground truth, ARKitScenes visit 421380; third-party data, see docs/PUBLIC_VALIDATION.md).

## 1. Worst-performing gate

- **Ceiling height, LiDAR tier:** gate ≤ 1.5 cm per room.
- **Failing number:** recording 42445021 measures **2.3338 m** against a laser ground truth of **2.3149 m**, an error of **+1.89 cm**.
- **Why this gate:** the room-segmentation attempt (notes D21) turned out to be scored against a ground truth with an assumed wall, so it was the wrong target. Ceiling height is fully observed by the laser, with no assumptions.

## 2. Root-cause hypothesis and evidence

**Hypothesis:** floor and ceiling heights are estimated on the fused cloud after **2 cm voxel averaging**. Averaging the points inside each 2 cm cell quantizes their heights into layers, and the peak-bin + trimmed-mean estimator reads a biased plane height from that quantized distribution.

**Evidence** (`tools/ceiling_diag.py`):

| | floor | ceiling | height | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw LiDAR points (confidence 2, no voxels) | −1.5220 | 0.7942 | 2.3162 | +0.13 cm |
| pipeline, 2 cm voxel-averaged cloud | −1.5356 | 0.7982 | 2.3338 | +1.89 cm |

- The fused cloud's 1 cm height histogram is a comb (10.8k, 5.0k, 11.8k, 1.7k, 6.7k). That pattern isn't physical; it's voxel quantization.
- The raw points' histogram is smooth.
- **The sensor is not the cause:** raw data agree with the laser to 1.3 mm.
- **Alternatives ruled out:**
  - *Range- or angle-dependent LiDAR bias on the ceiling.* Ceiling plane height varies by ≤ 0.4 cm across 0–3 m range and 40–75° incidence.
  - *A rug pulling the floor estimate.* There is a second floor surface 4 cm up, but the estimator already picks the lower peak.

## 3. Fix and predicted number

- **Fix:** the LiDAR tier keeps a raw, non-voxelized subsample of horizontal-surface points. Plane heights (whole scan and per room) are estimated on those; walls and everything else still use the fused cloud.
- **Predicted:** ceiling error **+1.89 cm → within ±0.5 cm**, so the gate flips **fail → pass**.
- **Predicted side effects:** none on the 9-room sample apartment's plan (same room count and areas).

## 4. Result

Regenerated from the tags with `python tools/fixloop_ceiling.py` (one pipeline pass at a time, then `report`). Laser ceiling height: 2.3149 m.

| room | `fix-before` | `fix-after` | gate ≤ 1.5 cm |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 (7.2 m², main part of the room) | 2.3300 (+1.51 cm) | 2.3140 (**−0.09 cm**) | FAIL → **PASS** |
| R2 (3.3 m², rest of the same room) | 2.3450 (+3.01 cm) | 2.3370 (**+2.21 cm**) | FAIL → FAIL |

The 9-room sample apartment is identical at both tags (8 rooms, 63.28 m², same room areas): no side effects.

**Prediction vs result.**
- **R1:** the predicted ±0.5 cm held (−0.09 cm).
- **The gate as a whole:** it is per room, and **R2 still fails**, so the gate has not flipped.
- **The declared failing number:** +1.89 cm was measured on an earlier commit (`ea49c2d`). At the `fix-before` tag the room polygons differ slightly (segmentation change, notes D21), giving +1.51 cm for R1. The direction and the fail verdict are the same.

**Root cause: partly right.**
- **Correct:** the voxel-averaged cloud was the culprit, and moving plane heights to raw points removed most of the error.
- **Wrong:** the declared *mechanism*, quantization into voxel layers. An isolation test on the same recording separated the causes (notes D22):

| test | error |
|---|---|
| horizontal points voxelized on their own | +0.14 cm |
| all surfaces voxelized together | +2.03 cm |
| raw horizontal points (the fix) | +0.38 cm |

  So the bias comes from voxel cells **mixing** floor and ceiling points with walls and clutter, not from quantization. The shipped fix removes it either way, because it selects horizontal points before any averaging.

**Why R2 falls short:** not diagnosed yet. R2 uses room-local floor and ceiling estimates over a smaller area. That's the next step, and this section will be updated with the evidence.

Artifacts: `fixloop/CEILING_DELTA.md`, `fixloop/code.diff` (regenerated by the tool; `git diff fix-before fix-after -- roomscan` shows the same diff).

## 5. Iteration 2: declared before the code

**Remaining failure:** R2 ceiling **+2.21 cm** at `fix-after` (gate ≤ 1.5 cm per room).

**Root-cause hypothesis:** vertical drift in the ARKit poses. The 2D drift solver (x, z, yaw) assumed height drift was negligible (notes D9), and that assumption is wrong.

**Evidence** (raw level points; median offset from the whole-scan floor/ceiling, by time window):

| frames | R1 floor | R2 floor | R1 ceiling | R2 ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–361 | −0.51 | – | −1.99 | – |
| 361–718 | +1.19 | +1.22 | −0.27 | +0.71 |
| 718–1075 | – | – | +0.78 | +1.56 |
| 1075–1432 | +3.61 | +3.44 | +3.13 | +2.45 |
| 1432–1789 | +3.35 | +3.55 | – | – |
| 1789–2146 | +3.44 | – | −0.25 | – |
| 2146–2503 | +0.28 | +0.08 | −1.44 | – |
| 2503–2860 | +0.13 | +0.12 | −2.18 | −2.53 |

- **The offsets follow time, not place.** Floor *and* ceiling in *both* rooms read +3 to +3.6 cm high during frames 1075–1789.
- R2's ceiling was seen mostly during the drifted windows, and its floor mostly after the drift had returned, which inflates R2's height.
- **The laser rules out a real ceiling step:** its local height is 2.3143 m over R2 and 2.3157 m over R1.

**Fix:** floor-anchored vertical correction for LiDAR poses. Per time window, measure where that window's own floor points sit relative to the whole-scan floor, then shift those frames' poses vertically by that offset, interpolating in time where a window doesn't see the floor. It's part of drift handling, so it's off under `--no-drift`.

**Predicted:**
- the floor offsets per time window drop to within ±0.5 cm;
- R2 ceiling **+2.21 → within ±0.7 cm** and R1 within ±0.5 cm, so **both rooms pass and the gate flips**;
- the apartment's room count and areas are unchanged.

**Tags:** before = `fix-after` (iteration 1), after = `fix-after-2`.

### Iteration 2 result

Regenerated with `python tools/fixloop_ceiling.py run fix-after-2 target|apartment` and then `report`.

| room | `fix-before` | `fix-after` | `fix-after-2` | gate ≤ 1.5 cm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | +1.51 cm | −0.09 cm | **−1.29 cm** | PASS |
| R2 | +3.01 cm | +2.21 cm | **−0.59 cm** | **PASS** |

**Gate: FAIL → PASS** (every room ≤ 1.5 cm).

**Prediction vs result.**
- ✅ **Floor offsets per time window:** within ±0.4 cm (were −0.5 to +3.6 cm), as predicted.
- ✅ **R2:** −0.59 cm, within the predicted ±0.7 cm.
- ❌ **R1:** predicted ±0.5 cm, got −1.29 cm. It passes, with 0.2 cm margin. One window at the end of the walk (frames 2503–2860) still has its ceiling 2.1–2.9 cm low while its floor is correct. That isn't pose drift (drift would shift both). It matches the far, grazing ceiling views flagged in the first diagnosis (range 3–6 m: −2.3 cm), which drift had previously masked. **Next candidate:** range-weighted or near-range-only ceiling estimation.
- ❌ **Apartment "unchanged":** still 8 rooms, but the footprint is 63.28 → 63.82 m² (+0.9%) and five room areas shift by 0.1–0.5 m². Corrected pose heights change which points fall in the wall/floor height bands. There's no ground truth for the apartment, so better or worse is unknown.

**Lesson recorded:** D9's assumption that ARKit height drift is negligible was wrong. It is ~3 cm over a 48 s walk here.
